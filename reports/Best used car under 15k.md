# Nearly-new Hyundais beat Polos at £15,000

The best used-car deal for this buyer in October 2026 is a **2025-plate Hyundai i20 with about 7,500 miles at £14,549** from Motorpoint. It is the same size as the Polo they share now. It scored **98.1% in What Car?'s 2025 reliability survey**, sells for about **£6,450 under its new list price**, and keeps Hyundai's **transferable five-year, unlimited-mileage warranty until about 2030**. Close behind are a **2025 Hyundai i10 with 1,949 miles at £14,549**, from the only small-car line to score 100% in that survey, and a **nearly new 2024 Kia Picanto with 3,641 miles at £13,498**, which carries Kia's seven-year warranty. Kia's Ceed and Toyota's Yaris Hybrid are both very dependable, but at this money they buy older cars with more miles. Both make sense only if the buyer needs a bigger car (Ceed) or lower fuel bills (Yaris). Paying cash is the right call. Typical dealer PCP at **9.9–12.4% APR adds about £3,000–£4,000** to the cost of owning a £12,500 car over four years. Finance only comes out ahead when it carries a subsidy or deposit contribution that you can take and then settle early, and the used-car offers available this autumn are too small for that to be worth much. Buying from a large retailer with a 14-day money-back guarantee, and paying the deposit by credit card, removes most of the scam risk before any checks start. One caveat: every listing below comes from search-result snapshots taken on 20 October 2026. None could be opened directly, so check each one is still live before acting.

## 2025-plate Hyundais give the most car per pound

The market splits clearly by brand. Hyundai, Suzuki and Kia lose value quickly, so £14–15k buys 2025 cars with fewer than 12,000 miles. Toyota and Honda hold their value, so the same money buys a 2023 car with 15,000–40,000 miles. A 2023 Yaris Hybrid averages **£17,662 used, with a median of 20,600 miles** ([AutoUncle](https://www.autouncle.co.uk/en-gb/used-cars/Toyota/Yaris%20Hybrid/y-2023)). The cheapest 2023 Honda Jazz Hybrid found was **£16,499** ([Motorpoint](https://www.motorpoint.co.uk/vehicle-details/honda-jazz/1665740)). A buyer who wants the newest registration and the lowest mileage should therefore look past the Japanese brands' reputations. The Korean cars score as well or better on reliability anyway.

The **i20 ranks first because it meets all four of the buyer's priorities with no compromise.** It is a 2025 car with single-digit thousands of miles. It has a top-quartile reliability score, and What Car? calls it "top-notch" ([What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/hyundai/i20/hatchback/used-review/n25790/reliability)). It is the same size class as the Polo, so it suits a shared household car. Motorpoint listed a second 2025 i20 at **£14,599 with 10,229 miles**, and Lookers had a 2024 i20 N Line automatic with **4,777 miles at £15,495**, slightly over budget ([Lookers](https://www.lookers.co.uk/used-cars/used-hyundai/i20)). Used i20 values are falling: they are **down 11.8% year on year** ([CarGurus](https://www.cargurus.co.uk/Cars/l-Used-Hyundai-i20-d3237)), which favours buyers. The i20 fuel-pump recall covers only cars built between April 2021 and January 2023 ([Honest John](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/news/owning/2025-10/dvsa-uk-recall-round-up-october-2025-hyundai-ford-and-mazda-models-recalled/)), so it does not affect 2025 stock.

The **i10 is the safest choice on paper.** The 2020-on model was one of only four cars in the entire 2025 What Car? survey to score 100% ([Autocar](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/slideshow/most-reliable-cars-2025-%E2%80%93-and-least)). The 1,949-mile example is effectively a new car at **£3,300 below list**. The downside is size. It is a city car, a class below the Polo, and the Picanto at rank 3 is the same size. The Picanto's best argument is cost: £13,498 for a nearly new car with about five years of Kia warranty left. It also comes top among cheap-to-fix cars in Warrantywise's claims data, which is based on **about 1.6 million real repair requests** ([Warrantywise](https://www.warrantywise.co.uk/reliability-index-2026/); [Wheels-Alive](https://www.wheels-alive.co.uk/toyota-yaris-comes-out-top-in-the-latest-warrantywise-used-car-reliability-index/)). The listing's 73/24 plate may be a pre-facelift car, but the older model's 97.2% still beats the small-car class average.

Two cars sit just outside the top five. A **2025 Suzuki Swift mild hybrid with 3,810 miles at £14,449** costs £5,445 under list ([Motorpoint](https://www.motorpoint.co.uk/vehicle-details/suzuki-swift/1632004)). Suzuki is What Car?'s most reliable brand at 97.8% ([What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-cars-brands/n26159)), but the current-generation Swift has no model-level score of its own yet. A **2024 VW Polo 1.0 Life with 7,398 miles costs £14,999** at Motorpoint, and a 2024 Skoda Fabia SE L with 12,733 miles costs £14,799 ([Motorpoint Fabia](https://www.motorpoint.co.uk/used-cars/skoda/fabia/se-l)). Both are reliable and familiar, but they are a year older than the Hyundais for the same money, and their factory warranty runs out sooner: the Polo's ends around April 2027, and the Fabia listing shows 12 months left.

## Survey and claims data back Hyundai, Kia and Toyota

Two kinds of evidence point the same way. What Car?'s 2025 survey asked **about 32,000 owners** about cars up to five years old. It puts the small-car average at **95.8%** and advises choosing petrol over electric for a dependable small car ([What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/news/most-reliable-small-cars/n26142)). Warrantywise's 2026 index covers cars aged 3–15 years. It ranks the **Yaris 1st, the Picanto 2nd and the Ceed and Swift joint 9th**, and no Ford, VW Group, Renault, Dacia or MG model makes its top ten ([Motoring Research](https://www.motoringresearch.com/car-news/10-most-reliable-used-cars-2026/)). Honest John's 2025 owner index adds the **Honda Jazz at 96.81%**, the most reliable small car it covers ([Honest John](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/the-honest-john-satisfaction-index/the-honest-john-satisfaction-index-cars/)).

The Yaris is the one model where the sources disagree, and the reason matters. It scores **92.5% in What Car? (20th of 28)**, but **22% of its faults are a flat 12V battery, 77% of owners paid nothing, and 65% were off the road for less than a day** ([What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/toyota/yaris/hatchback/used-review/n24498/reliability)). So it breaks down often in minor ways, not expensively. The flip side shows in Warrantywise's figures: when a Yaris does need work, the **average repair is £889, more than double the Picanto's £403** ([Wheels-Alive](https://www.wheels-alive.co.uk/toyota-yaris-comes-out-top-in-the-latest-warrantywise-used-car-reliability-index/)). The Polo's reported **98.4%** comes from a search extract only, with the survey year unconfirmed, so treat it as indicative.

Several cars that look cheap for their age should be avoided. The **Renault Clio (2019-on)** scores 93.7% in What Car? and has the **lowest MOT pass rate of any high-volume model, 53.87%** across all ages ([Brumble](https://brumble.co.uk/tools/mot-checker)). The **Dacia Sandero** scores 93.1% ([What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-small-cars/n26142)), and a nearly new entry-level one is poor value when a **new Sandero starts at £14,730** ([Carwow](https://www.carwow.co.uk/dacia/sandero)). The **MG3** sits near the bottom of the small-car table at 87.9% ([Autocar](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/slideshow/most-reliable-cars-2025-%E2%80%93-and-least)). That figure may describe the older petrol model rather than the 2024 Hybrid+, but a **2024 MG3 Hybrid+ with 6,402 miles at £12,995** ([Stoneacre](https://www.stoneacre.co.uk/used-cars/mg/3-hybridplus)) still only suits a buyer willing to rely on its seven-year warranty instead of a proven record. The **Ford Puma** scores 91.4%, 32nd of 38 small SUVs, and Parkers calls a **VIN check "vital"** because early cars may have the 1.0 EcoBoost wet timing belt ([What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/ford/puma/hatchback/used-review/n22465/reliability); [Parkers](https://www.parkers.co.uk/car-news/ford-wet-belt-issues/)).

If the buyer still wants a VW Group car (Polo, Ibiza or Fabia), **choose the manual**. Every automatic Polo since 2017 uses the DQ200 dry-clutch DSG, which wears faster in stop-start city driving ([minbilkoll.se](https://minbilkoll.se/vanliga-fel/volkswagen/polo-5-dorrar/)).

## Big retailers' guarantees do half the anti-scam work

Most of the strongest listings come from Motorpoint, which affects both price and risk. Motorpoint says its prices are "benchmarked daily" against the main marketplace ([Motorpoint](https://www.motorpoint.co.uk/vehicle-details/hyundai-i10/1618094)). It offers a **14-day money-back guarantee on every purchase, in store or online**, plus a free three-month warranty on any car no longer under its manufacturer warranty ([Motorpoint](https://www.motorpoint.co.uk/blog/what-is-the-motorpoint-extended-warranty/505973)). Cinch matches the 14-day return window ([cinch](https://www.cinch.co.uk/returns)). The prices found also look normal for the market. Motorpoint's 2024 Fabias sit at the low-to-mid end of AutoUncle's **£13,281–£18,995** range for that year ([AutoUncle](https://www.autouncle.co.uk/en-gb/used-cars/Skoda/Fabia/y-2024)). The cheapest 2023 Yaris Hybrids (£12,000–£13,000 at the AA) are cheap because they have **63,000–67,000 miles**, not because something is wrong ([AA](https://www.theaa.com/used-cars/toyota/yaris/2023)).

That gives rough fair-value benchmarks for October 2026: **£13.8k–£15.5k** for a 2025 i10, i20, Swift or Sandero, **£13.9k–£15k** for a 2024 Fabia or Polo, and **£14.5k–£16k** for a 2023 Yaris Hybrid with normal mileage. A 2024–25 car from these brands advertised far below these figures should be treated as a write-off, a car with outstanding finance, a clocked car or a scam until checks prove otherwise. One aggregator showed a **2025 Sandero Stepway at about £5,400** in a market averaging £15,787 ([CarGurus](https://www.cargurus.co.uk/Cars/l-Used-2025-Dacia-Sandero-Stepway-South-East-England-c65624_L948712)). That is exactly the pattern Action Fraud warns about ([Action Fraud](https://actionfraud.police.uk/news/buyers-beware-of-online-car-scam)).

## Cash beats 10% dealer finance by about £3,000

Interest rates rule out cheap PCP this autumn. **Bank Rate has been 3.75% since December 2025**, and three MPC members voted to *raise* it in September 2026 ([MTS Insights](https://www.mtsinsights.com/summaries/10990/)). Manufacturer approved-used finance costs **9.9% APR at Toyota and 12.4% at VW** ([Toyota](https://www.toyota.co.uk/used-cars); [VW](https://www.volkswagen.co.uk/en/used-cars/approved-used-offers.html)). Used-car supermarkets average **12.15%** ([Carsa](https://www.carsa.co.uk/car-finance)), and Motorpoint advertises **10.9–11.9%**. The table below uses the research team's own calculations for a £12,500 car over 48 months with a £1,250 deposit. They are not lender quotes, and the PCP balloon is assumed at 35% of the price.

| Option | Monthly | Total cost to own | Extra vs cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | – | £12,500 | – |
| Personal loan at 6.4% (best rate, Santander/Tesco, [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/loans/best-personal-loan-deals/10000-loans-over-five-years-adgjw4k4d8z8)) | £265 | £13,986 | +£1,486 |
| HP at 9.9% | £283 | £14,811 | +£2,311 |
| PCP at 9.9%, pay balloon to keep | £207 | £15,571 | **+£3,071** |
| PCP at 12.9%, pay balloon to keep | £226 | £16,481 | **+£3,981** |
| PCP at 0% with £1,000 deposit contribution | £122 | £11,500 | −£1,000 |

PCP's lower monthly payment costs more overall, because **interest builds up on the balloon for the whole term**. The 0% row shows the only real exception: a manufacturer subsidy that cash buyers cannot get. The research found no such used-car offer for these models in October 2026. Used-car deposit contributions found are just **£250 at VW and £500 on Kia's EV6** ([Kia](https://www.kia.com/uk/used-cars/used-car-offers/ev6/)). Even if the buyer takes the contribution and settles within a month, the gain is only about **£150–£450**. No Hyundai approved-used finance offer was found at all.

PCP also has costs at the end of the deal. Consumers quote excess-mileage charges of **7–11p a mile** in their contracts, damage beyond fair wear and tear is charged, and voluntary termination is only available once half the total amount payable has been paid ([MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5180860/pcp-voluntary-termination-excess-mileage)). The commission redress scheme does not help either: it excludes agreements signed after 1 November 2024, and its payouts are suspended pending an Upper Tribunal hearing ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/statements/motor-finance-scheme-partially-suspended)).

**Verdict: pay cash.** The one honest case for borrowing is if the cash also serves as an emergency fund. In that case a 6.4% personal loan roughly breaks even with savings interest at an assumed 4% (an assumption, not a sourced savings rate), and it costs about £1,600–£2,500 less than dealer PCP. Whichever way the buyer pays, **put £100 or more of the deposit on a credit card**. That brings the whole purchase price under Section 75 protection, which covers cash prices from £100 to £30,000 ([Zable](https://app.zable.co.uk/cards/how-do-credit-cards-work/can-you-buy-a-car-with-a-credit-card)).

## Nine checks shut out most used-car scams

Most of the risk is in social-media listings. **Vehicles were the largest purchase-scam category reported to Lloyds in 2025**, and more than 70% of purchase scams started on Meta platforms ([Jalopnik citing Lloyds](https://www.jalopnik.com/2236283/facebook-marketplace-reverse-image-search-why/)). Hidden history is also common in the cars people check. HPI reports that **about 1 in 3 cars it checks has outstanding finance and 1 in 11 has a mileage discrepancy** ([HPI](https://hpicheck.com/car-history-check)). These are shares of cars checked, not of all used cars. The checklist below covers both risks, in the order a buyer meets them.

| # | Check | How | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shop where you can view and return | Franchised dealers or Motorpoint, Arnold Clark or cinch, over Marketplace or Gumtree. Compare the price with the benchmarks above | Dealer sales come with a **30-day right to reject and a 6-month presumption that faults were there at sale** ([Honest John](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/how-to-reject-a-car-your-consumer-rights)). Private sales do not |
| 2 | Pay nothing before viewing | Refuse "holding deposits", couriers, "seller abroad" stories and any escrow or "holding account" the seller suggests | Fake escrow (including fake Amazon-branded accounts) is a known car scam ([Action Fraud](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk/news/fake-holding-accounts-target-car-buyers-online)) |
| 3 | Run the free gov.uk checks | DVLA vehicle enquiry, MOT history and the recall checker ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/checks-when-buying-a-used-car); [recalls](https://www.gov.uk/check-if-a-vehicle-has-been-recalled)) | Catches identity mismatches and unfixed recalls. **A 2024–25 car has no MOT mileage record yet**, so the service record and paid check have to do that job |
| 4 | Buy a paid history check on the final car | HPI £19.99, Green Flag £9.99, CarAnalytics £14.99 ([CarAnalytics](https://www.caranalytics.co.uk/guides/who-has-the-cheapest-hpi-check/)). Run it on the day you buy | The only practical way to see outstanding finance, Cat S/N write-off and stolen markers |
| 5 | Match the car to its documents | VIN on the car = V5C = history check. V5C watermark. View at the keeper's address ([HPI](https://hpicheck.com/car-buyers-guide/guides/the-paperwork)) | Cloned cars carry real-looking plates. DVLA received a **record 11,402 complaints about fines for unrecognised vehicles in 2025** ([RAC](https://www.rac.co.uk/drive/news/motoring-news/cloned-vehicles-rise-to-record-high/)) |
| 6 | Spot disguised traders | A "private" seller with many cars for sale, or who wants to meet in car parks or lay-bys ([Highland Council](https://www.highland.gov.uk/news/article/2243/trading_standards_clamp_down_on_disguised_car_dealers)) | A trader posing as private takes away your Consumer Rights Act protection |
| 7 | Get an inspection for any private sale | AA inspections cost **£99–£339** ([AA](https://www.theaa.com/vehicle-inspections/)) | About 1–2% of the price, and a private sale gives little legal comeback |
| 8 | Pay safely | Credit card for the deposit (Section 75). For a bank transfer, accept only a Confirmation of Payee "match" ([UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/policy-and-guidance/guidance/confirmation-payee)) | Fraud by bank transfer is reimbursable up to **£85,000** if claimed within 13 months, but ignoring bank warnings can void the claim ([Money to the Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/banking/what-are-the-new-app-fraud-reimbursement-rules)) |
| 9 | Know your cooling-off rights | A fully remote dealer purchase gives **14 days from delivery** to return the car ([Motor Ombudsman](https://www.themotorombudsman.org/knowledge/can-i-return-my-car-within-14-days-of-buying-it/)). Finance has its own 14-day cooling-off | Visiting the dealer to pay or sign cancels the remote-purchase right ([Motorway](https://motorway.co.uk/sell-my-car/guides/is-there-a-cooling-off-period-when-buying-a-car)). Motorpoint's own 14-day guarantee covers in-store purchases too |

## Conclusion

The common advice to "buy a Toyota or Honda" points this buyer the wrong way. Their value holds up so well that the same £15,000 buys a car two years older with three to five times the mileage. Hyundai and Kia give equal or better survey reliability, more warranty years and the newest plates for the money. The buyer's four priorities, which looked like they might pull against each other, all point to the same answer: a nearly new Korean supermini bought from a large retailer. The things still worth doing yourself are a £10–£20 history check and a quick look at whether a new car costs about the same. For a nearly new car, asking whether new is the same price is as useful as any reliability table.

Two uncertainties remain. First, none of the listings could be confirmed as live on 20 October 2026, and stock at this price sells within days. Expect to find the *type* of car described here, not necessarily the exact car. Second, the 2026 What Car? model tables were not available, and Which? data is paywalled. A fresh survey could change the order between the i20, i10 and Picanto. It is very unlikely to lift the Clio, Sandero, MG3 or Puma above them.
