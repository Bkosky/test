# Reliability evidence for UK used small and family cars (2019–2024 model years)

> **Method note for the report writer:** The network blocked direct page fetches to whatcar.com, warrantywise.co.uk and the other primary sites (proxy denial), so I could not open them. Every figure below comes from search-engine extracts of those pages. Each figure is cited to the page it came from. Where extracts conflict, I say so. Before publishing, treat any figure that appears only once as "reported, not verified line by line". Dataset years are given for every figure. As of Oct 2026, the latest What Car? model-level data I could confirm is the **2025 survey**. I could not confirm a 2026 edition of the model tables (see Gaps).

## 1. What do What Car?, Which? and Honest John say (scores, fault rates, repair costs)?

### Takeaway
Across the independent surveys, the most consistent top performers are the Toyota Yaris, Kia Picanto, Hyundai i10/i20, Honda Jazz, Kia Ceed and Suzuki Swift. The Renault Clio, Dacia Sandero, Ford Puma, MG ZS and the older (2015–2021) Skoda Fabia sit lower in the tables. The VW Polo and the current Fabia score well in What Car?'s latest data. The Yaris is strong overall, but its 12V battery is a recurring niggle.

### Cited Findings

**What Car? Reliability Survey 2025 (cars up to 5 years old, run with MotorEasy; about 32,000 owners)**
- Small-car class average **95.8%**, nearly 1 point higher than in the 2024 survey. **Hyundai i10 (2020-on) scored 100%**, with the Toyota Aygo X and the 2014–2024 Mini close behind. The lowest scorers were EVs (Renault Zoe and Vauxhall Corsa Electric), and What Car? advises choosing petrol for a dependable small car. — [What Car? small cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/most-reliable-small-cars/n26142)
- Renault Zoe (2013–2024) was the least reliable small car at 85.1%, with a 39% fault rate. The survey covered about 32,000 owners. — [Autocar summary of 2025 survey](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/slideshow/most-reliable-cars-2025-%E2%80%93-and-least); [What Car? small cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-small-cars/n26142)
- **Toyota Yaris (2020-on):** 92.5%, 20th of 28 small cars in the 2025 survey. 25% fault rate, and 22% of faults were the 12V battery. 77% of owners paid £0 and 65% were off the road for less than a day. — [What Car? used Yaris reliability](https://www.whatcar.com/toyota/yaris/hatchback/used-review/n24498/reliability). **Conflict:** another What Car? extract puts the Yaris 13th with a 20% fault rate (the survey year is unclear). — [What Car? small cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-small-cars/n26142)
- Yaris: Toyota paid for 69% of remedial work, and some owners faced bills of up to £200. Owners also reported hybrid-system warning lights that needed dealer visits. — [What Car? used Yaris reliability](https://www.whatcar.com/toyota/yaris/hatchback/used-review/n24498/reliability)
- **Honda Jazz (2020-on):** 96.9%, 11th of 28 small cars. The previous generation (2015–2020) scored 98.7% in the 2022 survey, when only 6% of owners reported problems. — [What Car? used Jazz reliability](https://www.whatcar.com/honda/jazz/hatchback/used-review/n24519/reliability); [What Car? 2022 survey](https://car-leasing.whatcar.com/news/2022-what-car-reliability-survey/n23397)
- **Hyundai i20 (2020-on):** 98.1%, 6th of 24 small cars. What Car? calls its reliability "top-notch". — [What Car? used i20 reliability](https://www.whatcar.com/hyundai/i20/hatchback/used-review/n25790/reliability)
- **Kia Picanto:** the 2024-on facelift is joint 4th at 98.4%, with 9% of cars reporting any issue. **The 2017–2024 model scored 97.2%.** — [What Car? small cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-small-cars/n26142)
- **Suzuki Swift (2017–2024):** 95.9%. Across the brand, 17% of Suzukis went wrong and 42% of faults were fixed within a day. An older What Car? list had the Swift at 100%, probably from an earlier survey year. — [What Car? small cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-small-cars/n26142)
- **VW Polo (2018-on):** reported at 98.4%, ahead of the Fiesta, MG3 and Clio. — [What Car? small cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-small-cars/n26142) (search extract only, survey year not explicit)
- **Ford Fiesta (2017–2023):** 95.2%. That puts it below the Jazz, Mini and Polo, and above the Peugeot 208, Clio and Fabia. What Car? describes its reliability as "mixed". — [What Car? used Fiesta reliability](https://www.whatcar.com/ford/fiesta/hatchback/used-review/n952/reliability)
- **Renault Clio (2019-on):** 93.7%, 18th. **Dacia Sandero (2021-on):** 93.1%, 19th. — [What Car? small cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-small-cars/n26142)
- **Skoda Fabia:** the 2021-on model came 6th of 26 at 97.5%. The 2015–2021 model scored 88.6%, partly because Skoda paid for only 43% of fault repairs. — [What Car? used Fabia 2021-on reliability](https://www.whatcar.com/skoda/fabia/hatchback/used-review/n26317/reliability); [What Car? older small cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/best-and-worst-older-small-cars-for-reliability/n19263)
- **Mazda2 (2015-on):** 94.7%, joint 12th in the **2022** survey, which is older data. — [What Car? 2022 survey](https://car-leasing.whatcar.com/news/2022-what-car-reliability-survey/n23397)
- **Family cars:** the **Kia Ceed (2018–2025) topped the class at 98.8%**, with only 8% reporting issues, and those were sat-nav/infotainment only. **Toyota Corolla (2018-on) scored 96.5%, joint 6th.** — [What Car? family cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-family-cars/n26149)
- Hyundai i30 (2017-on) was shown at 98% in a 2023 UK used-car list. The 2012–2017 i30 scored 94.9% in What Car?'s used survey. Mazda3 (2014–2019 petrol) scored 99.3% in an older used survey. No current-survey figures turned up for the 2019-on Mazda3 or the i30. — [What Car? used family cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/most-and-least-reliable-used-cars-family-cars/n19129); [lovemoney 2023](https://www.lovemoney.com/news/46447/most-least-reliable-used-family-cars-uk-best-worst)
- **Small SUVs:** the class average is 93.7%. **Ford Puma (2019-on) scored 91.4%, 32nd of 38** (in the 2023 survey it scored 90.0%, 17th of 22). **MG ZS petrol/PHEV scored 91.6%, 31st.** The MG ZS EV scored 81.8% in the family-SUV table and 94.5% in the electric-SUV table, so the figures conflict. — [What Car? used Puma reliability](https://www.whatcar.com/ford/puma/hatchback/used-review/n22465/reliability); [What Car? small SUVs](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-small-suvs/n26154); [What Car? petrol cars](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-and-least-reliable-petrol-cars/n26745)
- **Brands (cars up to 5 years old, 2025 survey):** Suzuki 1st at 97.8%, Honda 2nd at 95.8%, Mini 3rd at 95.4%. — [What Car? brands](https://www.whatcar.com/news/reliability-survey-most-reliable-cars-brands/n26159)
- In What Car? Car of the Year 2026 (published early 2026), the Reliability Award went to **Honda**. Its best model was the HR-V at 97.5%, followed by the Civic at 97.3%. — [What Car? COTY 2026 Reliability Award](https://www.whatcar.com/news/car-year-awards-2026-reliability-award/n25377)

**Honest John Satisfaction Index 2025 (owner reliability scores)**
- **Honda Jazz 96.81%**, 4th overall and the most reliable small car. One HJ page gives 96.84%. **Kia Picanto 94.09%** (13th) and **Toyota Yaris 93.57%** (16th). The Suzuki Swift is not in HJ's top 20. — [Honest John Satisfaction Index](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/the-honest-john-satisfaction-index/the-honest-john-satisfaction-index-cars/); [HJ most reliable small cars 2026](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/guides/reliable-cars/most-reliable-small-cars/)

**Auto Express Driver Power 2026**
- The Toyota Aygo X ranked 1st among small cars, followed by the Mazda CX-30. — [What Car?/Auto Express search extract](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/driver-power/86752/top-10-most-reliable-small-cars-buy-20212022) (model-level detail for the target cars was not retrieved)

### Inferences
- In What Car?'s 2025 data, the newer Asian superminis (i10, i20, Picanto, Jazz) and the VW Polo and current Fabia score at or above the 95.8% class average. The Yaris (92.5%), Clio (93.7%) and Sandero (93.1%) score below it. The Yaris's lower score is driven by a cheap but frequent fault (the 12V battery, with 77% paying nothing), not by expensive failures. So it ranks lower on "any fault" rate than on cost or severity.
- The Ford Puma and MG ZS are bottom-quartile small SUVs in What Car?'s data. A buyer who prioritises reliability would do better with a Ceed or Corolla in the family-size bracket.
- Generation matters a great deal. The Fabia scored 88.6% for 2015–2021 and 97.5% for 2021-on. The Picanto improved after 2024. The Jazz differs strongly between generations. Model-year ranges must be matched to survey entries.

### Gaps
- **Which?**: I found no Which? reliability figures in the search results. Which? data is paywalled and was not indexed. Model-level star ratings and fault rates are not available here.
- **What Car? 2026 survey**: I could not confirm whether model-level tables from a 2026 survey (normally published mid-year) exist. The figures above are from the 2025 survey or earlier.
- No current What Car? score was found for the Seat Ibiza, MG3, Kia Rio, Hyundai i30 (2017-on), Mazda3 (2019-on) or the Mazda2 Hybrid (a rebadged Yaris).
- The VW Polo 98.4% figure and the conflicting Yaris rank (13th/20% vs 20th/25%) could not be verified against the source tables.

## 2. What does MOT first-time pass rate data show at 3–5 years old?

### Takeaway
Recent model-level MOT data for 3–5-year-old cars in this group is thin in what I could reach. The clearest current signal is that the **Renault Clio has the lowest MOT pass rate of any high-volume model (53.87% in 2025, all ages)**. At age 3, though, most cars pass at about 87–89%, so MOT data separates models mainly at older ages. The Honda Jazz has historically topped the 3–5-year-old MOT tables.

### Cited Findings
- Brumble's analysis of the 2025 DVSA test year (published June 2026): a 3-year-old car has an 89% chance of passing. The **Renault Clio passed 53.87% of tests** (over 50,000 tests, all ages), the lowest of any model with 50k+ tests. Renault is the lowest major brand at 62.42%, and Porsche the highest at 87.49%. — [Brumble MOT checker](https://brumble.co.uk/tools/mot-checker)
- Brumble's Renault page reports the Clio at 53.9% across 246,985 tests, against a national average of 72.4%. **At age 3, Renaults pass at 87.2% vs 88.5% nationally**, a gap that "widens rapidly" with age. — [Brumble Renault report](https://brumble.co.uk/premium-vehicle-report/renault)
- Other trackers report the Clio at 68.2% for 2024 (502,539 tests) and 73% averaged across model years. The methods differ. — [motdata.uk Clio](https://motdata.uk/mot/renault-clio); [CarCostCheck Clio](https://www.carcostcheck.co.uk/car/renault-clio)
- Brumble's Peugeot page: "1 in 3 Peugeots fail their MOT". — [Brumble Peugeot report](https://brumble.co.uk/premium-vehicle-report/peugeot)
- Older data (2019 DVSA, cars aged 3–5 only): **Honda Jazz 95.4%, the most likely car to pass.** — [Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/news/105772/best-and-worst-cars-mot-pass-rates-revealed)
- An older BookMyGarage analysis of 2020 DVSA data on first MOTs found Honda and Mazda, along with Subaru and Lexus, above 90% among mainstream brands. — [Yorkshire Post / BookMyGarage](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/lifestyle/cars/want-the-best-chance-of-passing-your-mot-first-time-buy-a-ferrari-3275667)
- The national first-time pass rate is quoted inconsistently: about 78.1% (2024/25), about 72.5% (2025/26), and about 87% for under-5s. These figures depend on how the population is defined. — [MOT Checkup State of UK MOT 2026](https://www.motcheckup.co.uk/research/state-of-uk-mot-2026); [Kael Tripton](https://www.kaeltripton.com/mot-failure-rates-uk/)
- Among EVs at their first MOT (2025 data), the **electric Vauxhall Corsa failed 18.5%**, among the worst. — [CarCostCheck EV MOT](https://www.carcostcheck.co.uk/blog/electric-car-mot-failure-rates-2025)

### Inferences
- At 3 years old, MOT pass rates cluster at about 87–90% for most mainstream brands, so MOT data separates these models weakly at that age. It mostly reflects consumables (tyres, bulbs, wipers) and owner maintenance, not mechanical reliability.
- The Clio's poor all-age pass rate fits its below-average What Car? score. It is a mild negative for a 2019–2021 Clio at 5+ years.

### Gaps
- No 2024/2025 model-specific first-MOT (3-year) or 3–5-year pass rates were found for the Yaris, Jazz, Picanto, i10/i20, Swift, Polo, Fabia, Fiesta, Puma, Sandero, MG3/ZS or Corolla. The DVSA anonymised MOT dataset (data.gov.uk) would allow this, but I could not retrieve it.

## 3. Which engines, gearboxes and years have known faults to avoid or check?

### Takeaway
The big-ticket risks in this segment are: (1) **Ford 1.0 EcoBoost wet timing belt** (Fiesta to 2023, earlier Puma/Focus; the change to chain is unclear by year, so check by VIN); (2) **Stellantis 1.0/1.2 PureTech wet belt and oil consumption** (Peugeot 208, Vauxhall Corsa, Citroen C3), now covered by a 10-year/112,000-mile extended warranty if serviced on schedule; and (3) **VW Group DQ200 7-speed dry-clutch DSG** in auto Polo/Ibiza/Fabia. The Toyota and Honda hybrids' main issue is minor (12V battery).

### Cited Findings

**Ford 1.0 EcoBoost (Fiesta, Puma, Focus, EcoSport)**
- In the US, NHTSA upgraded its probe into the 1.0 EcoBoost to an engineering analysis covering about 135,551 vehicles (2014–17 Fiesta, 2015–18 Focus, 2018–21 EcoSport). The belt material can degrade and shed debris that clogs the oil-pump pick-up screen, causing oil starvation. Average failure was at about 70,000 miles, 98% of failures came before the 150,000-mile replacement interval, and failures occurred even with routine oil changes. Ford launched a global "customer satisfaction programme", which is not a safety recall. — [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/ford-10l-ecoboost-wet-timing-belt-investigation-deepens-due-to-the-risk-of-sudden-engine-failure-273757.html); [Ford Authority](https://fordauthority.com/2025/12/nhtsa-opens-investigation-into-failing-ford-1-0l-ecoboost-engines/)
- UK (Parkers): the wet belt affects the Fiesta (2008–2023), EcoSport, Focus (2011–2018), B-Max, C-Max and Mondeo. Parkers says the **Puma (2019-on), Fiesta (2017–2023) and Focus (2018–2025) "should all have timing chains fitted, but the earliest examples may have a wet belt, so a VIN check is vital".** — [Parkers Ford wet belt](https://www.parkers.co.uk/car-news/ford-wet-belt-issues/)
- **Conflict:** other UK guides list the 2019-on Puma as affected. A Puma review says the mild-hybrid engine uses a timing chain, but **a wet oil-pump belt remains** and is a long-term concern above about 100,000 km. — [Parkers](https://www.parkers.co.uk/car-news/ford-wet-belt-issues/); [carchecker.pro Puma 1.0 EcoBoost](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/ford_puma_1.0_ecoboost.html)
- The belt degrades faster with short trips and infrequent use. — [NTN-SNR TECHinfo 1.0 EcoBoost (UK)](https://ntn-snr.com/sites/default/files/2025-07/TECHinfo%201.0%20EcoBoost_UK.pdf)

**Stellantis PureTech 1.0/1.2 (Peugeot 208/2008, Vauxhall Corsa F, Citroen C3; for comparison)**
- Since March 2024, Peugeot, Vauxhall and Citroen UK cover 100% of parts and labour on previous-generation PureTech 1.0/1.2 timing-belt and oil-consumption issues for **up to 10 years or 112,000 miles**. A full, on-schedule service history (up to 3 months or 3,000 km late is tolerated) is required. — [Peugeot UK PureTech](https://www.peugeot.co.uk/owners/puretech-engines-solutions.html); [Vauxhall UK PureTech support](https://www.vauxhall.co.uk/maintenance-services/warranties-care/customer-support-for-puretech.html); [Citroen UK PureTech PDF](https://www.citroen.co.uk/content/dam/citroen/uk/b2c/maintain/Puretech-Message-V-2-2025.pdf)
- Owners can claim compensation for past repairs. The UK pages say for costs incurred between 1 Jan 2022 and 18 Mar 2024. The Stellantis press release says to 31 Dec 2024, so the sources conflict. — [Stellantis UK media](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/ds/press/stellantis-extends-compensation-policy-for-european-consumers-claims-on-previous-generations-of-puretech-1-0-and-1-2-engines); [Stellantis compensation platform](https://www.stellantis.com/en/contacts/puretech-compensation-platform)

**VW Group 1.0 TSI and DQ200 DSG (Polo, Ibiza, Fabia)**
- The DQ200 7-speed **dry-clutch** DSG is fitted to all automatic Polos from 2017. The main issue is clutch wear, and it is sensitive to stop-start city use. The 1.0 MPI (manual only) avoids it. — [minbilkoll.se (Swedish)](https://minbilkoll.se/vanliga-fel/volkswagen/polo-5-dorrar/)
- 1.0 TSI timing-chain stretch is reported at about 80,000–150,000 km (a German workshop source). — [Kfz-Dietrich](https://kfz-dietrich.com/blog/polo-vw-typische-probleme-odis-diagnose/)
- The well-known DQ200 mechatronic and clutch failures were concentrated in **2010–2014** Golf-era cars. — [autopartspro](https://www.autopartspro.co.uk/tips-advice/volkswagen-golf-years-to-avoid-a-guide-to-common-issues-and-problems-10797)

**Toyota Yaris (hybrid) and others**
- 2020-on Yaris Hybrid: the commonest issue is a flat 12V battery if the car is used infrequently, plus some hybrid warning lights. — [What Car? used Yaris](https://www.whatcar.com/toyota/yaris/hatchback/used-review/n24498/reliability); [WhoCanFixMyCar](https://www.whocanfixmycar.com/advice/toyota-yaris-common-problems)
- On the older (2011–2020) Yaris, the Multidrive CVT is less reliable than the manual. — [Auto Express used Yaris Mk3](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/used-toyota-yaris-mk3-2011-2020-review-problems-recalls)

**Recalls (DVSA round-ups via Honest John)**
- **Hyundai i20**: 17,084 cars built **Apr 2021–Jan 2023** were recalled for a possible fuel-pump fault (pump replaced). — [HJ DVSA round-up Oct 2025](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/news/owning/2025-10/dvsa-uk-recall-round-up-october-2025-hyundai-ford-and-mazda-models-recalled/)
- **MG ZS**: 31,387 cars built **Nov 2020–Feb 2024** were recalled for an emissions issue. — [HJ DVSA round-up Dec 2025](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/news/owning/2025-12/dvsa-recall-round-up-mg-toyota-and-volkswagen-models-recalled/)
- **Ford Puma** (plus Kuga, Focus and others built Dec 2019–Apr 2024): recalled for loss of eCall emergency-call software function. — [HJ DVSA recalls May 2026](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/news/owning/2026-06/dvsa-recalls-may-2026-ford-hyundai-and-audi-models-recalled/)
- **Toyota Yaris Hybrid**: a hybrid-system software recall (Nov 2022) and an eCall recall. **Toyota Corolla**: instrument display recall. — [WhoCanFixMyCar](https://www.whocanfixmycar.com/advice/toyota-yaris-common-problems); [HJ DVSA round-up Dec 2025](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/news/owning/2025-12/dvsa-recall-round-up-mg-toyota-and-volkswagen-models-recalled/)

### Inferences
- **For a 2019–2023 Fiesta or early Puma with the 1.0 EcoBoost**, check by VIN whether it has a belt or a chain. Ask for evidence of the correct-spec oil (the Ford-spec oil for wet-belt engines) and of belt inspection or replacement. Price in a belt change unless it has been done. I found no cited UK cost for this job. Low-mileage, short-trip cars are higher risk.
- **For a PureTech 208, Corsa or C3**, the extended warranty substantially de-risks the belt, but only with a complete on-schedule service record. Without one, avoid.
- **For a VW Polo, Seat Ibiza or Skoda Fabia**, prefer the manual 1.0 TSI or MPI. If you buy the DSG, test for judder or hesitation when pulling away and check the gearbox software and service history.
- The Toyota, Honda, Hyundai/Kia and Suzuki models have no comparable engine-killing design fault in the findings above. Their main issues are recall-fixable (i20 fuel pump) or minor (Yaris 12V).

### Gaps
- No authoritative Ford statement was found giving the exact production date when the 1.0 EcoBoost (Fiesta Mk8 / Puma mHEV) switched from belt to chain. Sources conflict, so a VIN or dealer check is needed.
- No UK-specific evidence was found on Hyundai/Kia 48V mild-hybrid or iMT clutch faults, MG3 Hybrid+ issues, the Renault Clio E-Tech hybrid gearbox, the Dacia Sandero 1.0 TCe or the Suzuki Swift 1.2 mild-hybrid. These were not covered in the results.
- I did not query the full DVSA recall database. Recall lists above are from news round-ups and are not exhaustive. Check each car on gov.uk "Check if a vehicle has been recalled".

## 4. What do warranty providers' claims data show (Warrantywise, MotorEasy)?

### Takeaway
The **Warrantywise 2026 Used Car Reliability Index** is built on real repair claims for cars aged 3–15 years. It ranks the **Toyota Yaris 1st (89.2/100) and the Kia Picanto 2nd (86.8)**, with the **Kia Ceed and Suzuki Swift joint 9th (77.5)**. Toyota has four models in the top ten. The Picanto has the cheapest average repair of the top ten (about £403).

### Cited Findings
- Warrantywise Reliability Index 2026: based on about 1.6 million data points of real repair requests, cars aged 3–15 years, minimum 250 vehicles per model, UK only. — [Warrantywise Reliability Index 2026](https://www.warrantywise.co.uk/reliability-index-2026/); [Warrantywise top 10 for 2026](https://www.warrantywise.co.uk/blog/advice/the-top-10-most-reliable-used-vehicles-for-2026/)
- Top 10: **1 Toyota Yaris 89.2; 2 Kia Picanto 86.8; 3 Toyota Aygo 83.8; 4 Toyota RAV4 79.9**; then the Peugeot 108, Toyota Hilux, Citroen C1, Suzuki Vitara, **Kia Ceed 77.5 and Suzuki Swift 77.5** (joint 9th). — [Motoring Research](https://www.motoringresearch.com/car-news/10-most-reliable-used-cars-2026/); [Motor Trade News](https://www.motortradenews.com/used-cars/used-toyota-and-kia-models-top-warrantywise-reliability-index/)
- **Toyota Yaris**: average repair £888.90, highest single repair £2,334.55. **Kia Picanto**: average £403.49 (lowest in the top ten), highest £1,316.16. — [Wheels-Alive](https://www.wheels-alive.co.uk/toyota-yaris-comes-out-top-in-the-latest-warrantywise-used-car-reliability-index/)
- **Suzuki Swift**: average repair £723.39, highest £3,993.47. **Kia Ceed**: average £709.10, highest £5,069.30. — [Motoring Research](https://www.motoringresearch.com/car-news/10-most-reliable-used-cars-2026/)
- Bottom of the index: Land Rover Discovery 17.2, BMW 7 Series 18.7, Mercedes S-Class 19.2 and Audi A7 19.2, with an average bottom-ten repair cost of £2,051.63. — [Warrantywise lower scores](https://www.warrantywise.co.uk/blog/advice/the-used-cars-with-lower-reliability-scores-more-expensive-doesnt-always-mean-more-reliable/); [Motor Trader](https://www.motortrader.com/motor-trader-news/automotive-news/warrantywise-reveals-models-at-lower-end-of-reliability-index-07-05-2026)
- Average garage labour rates rose from £104.11/hr (2023) to £112.30/hr (2025). — [Warrantywise July 2026 report](https://www.warrantywise.co.uk/blog/advice/insights/july-2026-used-car-reliability-report/)
- In the 2025 index, Warrantywise had the Yaris at #1 and the Swift at #2. — [HJ / aggregator search extract](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/guides/reliable-cars/most-reliable-small-cars/) (secondary, not verified)
- Warrantywise cautions that a high score does not mean zero risk. — [Warrantywise Reliability Index 2026](https://www.warrantywise.co.uk/reliability-index-2026/)

### Inferences
- The Warrantywise data skews older (3–15 years) and toward simpler city cars. The Aygo, 108 and C1 trio does well partly because these cars are mechanically simple. The data still corroborates the owner surveys on the **Yaris, Picanto, Swift and Ceed**.
- The Yaris's average repair cost (£889) is about double the Picanto's (£403). This probably reflects costlier hybrid and electrical parts when something does fail.
- None of the Ford, VW Group, Renault, Dacia or MG models appear in the Warrantywise top ten.

### Gaps
- No Warrantywise scores were retrieved for the models outside the top and bottom ten (Jazz, i10/i20/i30, Mazda2/3, Fabia, Polo, Ibiza, Fiesta, Puma, Clio, Sandero, MG3/ZS, Corolla). The full index is on warrantywise.co.uk but could not be fetched.
- **MotorEasy**: no standalone MotorEasy reliability index was found. MotorEasy now co-runs the What Car? survey ([MotorEasy / What Car?](https://affl1.motoreasy.com/magazine/367/What-Car-Reliability-Survey)), so its data feeds into the What Car? figures above.
- Older ReliabilityIndex.com-style data (claims frequency and cost by model) was not found for the current period.
