## 3. Total cost over 3–4 years: cash vs PCP vs HP vs personal loan on £12,500 (worked example)

### Takeaway
On a £12,500 car over 48 months with a £1,250 deposit:

- **Cash** is cheapest on paper.
- **Personal loan (6.4%)** adds about £1,500.
- **HP at 9.9%** adds about £2,300.
- **PCP at 9.9%**, if you buy the car at the end, adds about £3,100. PCP costs more in total interest than HP because the balloon stays outstanding all term.

PCP only beats the alternatives when a manufacturer subsidy is present. With a low/0% APR plus a deposit contribution, total cost can fall *below* the cash price.

### Cited Findings (inputs)
- Typical captive used APR of 9.9% (Toyota FS) — [Toyota UK](https://www.toyota.co.uk/used-cars)
- Average used-supermarket APR of 12.15%; Carsa at 10.9% — [Carsa](https://www.carsa.co.uk/car-finance)
- Best personal loan of 6.4% APR representative (Santander/Tesco), Which? 2 September 2026 — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/loans/best-personal-loan-deals/10000-loans-over-five-years-adgjw4k4d8z8)
- PCP deposits are typically 10–20%; PCP balloon is paid to keep the car at the end — [Pocketwise](https://pocketwise.co.uk/debt/car-finance/car-finance-explained/)
- Example low-APR approved-used offers of 6.9% plus £500 deposit contribution (Kia) and 5.9% (Mazda dealer) — [Kia UK](https://www.kia.com/uk/used-cars/used-car-offers/ev6/); [RRG Mazda](https://www.rrg-group.com/mazda/used-cars/)

### Worked example (researcher's calculations, not lender quotes)

**Assumptions:**
- Cash price £12,500. Customer deposit £1,250 (10%), so £11,250 is financed. Term 48 months.
- PCP GFV/balloon assumed at £4,375 (35% of price). This is an assumption: GFVs on 3–5-year-old cars vary.
- Monthly payments are derived from APR as an effective annual rate.
- Option-to-purchase and admin fees are ignored. These are typically small (tens of pounds), but check the agreement.

| Option | APR | Monthly | Total payable (to own) | Cost vs cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | – | – | £12,500 | £0 (but see opportunity cost) |
| Personal loan (£11,250 + £1,250 own deposit) | 6.4% | £265.34 | £13,986 | +£1,486 |
| Personal loan (full £12,500, no deposit) | 6.4% | £294.82 | £14,152 | +£1,652 |
| HP | 9.9% | £282.51 | £14,811 | +£2,311 |
| HP | 12.9% | £297.33 | £15,522 | +£3,022 |
| HP | 14.9% | £307.25 | £15,998 | +£3,498 |
| PCP, pay balloon to keep | 9.9% | £207.20 | £15,571 | +£3,071 |
| PCP, pay balloon to keep | 12.9% | £226.16 | £16,481 | +£3,981 |
| PCP, hand car back | 9.9% | £207.20 | £11,196 paid, car returned | you own nothing |
| Manufacturer PCP: 6.9% + £1,000 deposit contribution, keep car | 6.9% | £164.24 | £13,508 (customer's money) | +£1,008 |
| Manufacturer PCP: 0% + £1,000 deposit contribution, keep car | 0% | £122.40 | £11,500 (customer's money) | **−£1,000 (beats cash)** |
| Take a £1,000 deposit contribution at 9.9%, settle after 1 month | 9.9% | £1 payment | about £11,661 (incl. one month of extra interest as a settlement charge) | **about −£840 (beats cash)** |

**Opportunity cost of cash:**
- If £12,500 sits in savings at an *assumed* 4% AER, it earns about £2,123 over 4 years, or about £1,678 after 20% tax. This rate is an assumption, broadly consistent with Bank Rate at 3.75%, but no savings-rate source was checked.
- On that basis, a 6.4% personal loan costs about £1,486 against forgone interest of about £1,700–£2,100 for a basic-rate taxpayer. The gap is small, roughly break-even to slightly favourable. Note that savings interest within the Personal Savings Allowance is tax-free.
- A 9.9%+ HP or PCP costs clearly more than the forgone savings interest.

### Inferences
- **The cost ranking for most buyers** is: cash ≈ personal loan (6.4%) < HP (about 10%) < PCP-to-own (about 10%). PCP's lower monthly payment is bought with higher total interest, because interest accrues on the balloon for the full term.
- **PCP genuinely beats cash in two cases:**
  1. A manufacturer subsidy (0% or very low APR) combined with a deposit contribution that is not available to cash buyers.
  2. Taking the finance-linked deposit contribution and then settling early.
- In both cases the buyer captures the contribution while paying little or no interest. A £1,000 contribution at 0% leaves the buyer about £1,000 better off than paying cash. At 6.9% with £1,000 contribution, the buyer is about £1,000 *worse* off than cash over 4 years unless they settle early.
- **Used-car contributions found in October 2026 are small** (£250 VW, £500 Kia). After 4 years of interest at 10–12%, they never beat cash. They only win if settled very early.
- **Hand-back PCP** (£11,196 for 4 years' use at 9.9% in the example) is a rental-style cost. The comparison here is with buying and later selling the car: cash-buyer depreciation of £12,500 minus resale value. If the car is worth more than the GFV at the end, the PCP buyer can also capture that equity by selling or part-exchanging.

### Gaps
- No current lender representative example for a £12.5k used car with actual GFV and fees was retrieved, because manufacturer pages were inaccessible. GFV % is assumed.
- No sourced current easy-access savings rate was found for the opportunity-cost calculation.
- The early-settlement charge is approximated as about one month of interest. UK rules allow lenders to charge up to 28 days' extra interest on early settlement (general Consumer Credit Act knowledge, not re-verified this session).

## 4. Motor finance commission scandal / FCA redress scheme (2024–2026) and what it means for buyers now

### Takeaway
The FCA finalised a compensation scheme on 30 March 2026. It covers about 12.1m agreements from 6 April 2007 to 1 November 2024 where the lender paid the broker commission, is worth about £9.1bn, and averages about £829–£830 per agreement.

The scheme is now partially suspended. Three lenders and a consumer group are challenging it before the Upper Tribunal, with a hearing likely from 14 December 2026 or 16 February 2027. Payments will begin in 2027 at the earliest.

For a buyer today, the scandal matters as background. Commission-linked rate-setting (discretionary commission arrangements, or DCAs) has been banned since January 2021. Commission paid to the dealer *still exists*, so buyers should ask about it and compare the dealer's APR against independent finance.

### Cited Findings
- **Supreme Court, Johnson v FirstRand (1 August 2025):**
  - Car dealers arranging finance do not owe customers fiduciary duties.
  - Non-disclosure of commission alone does not automatically make the relationship unfair.
  - Mr Johnson's s.140A Consumer Credit Act "unfair relationship" claim succeeded because the commission was 55% of the charge for credit, undisclosed, and there was an undisclosed contractual tie between dealer and lender.
  
  — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/08/12/supreme-court-rules-in-undisclosed-commissions-case-102kzma); [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/supreme-court-clarifies-legal-boundaries-in-motor-finance-commission-cases)
- **Industry exposure:** the Court of Appeal's earlier decision had prompted estimates of over £40bn in compensation. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/08/12/supreme-court-rules-in-undisclosed-commissions-case-102kzma)
- **Final rules:** the FCA consulted in October 2025 (CP25/27) on an £11bn scheme. Final rules were published 30 March 2026 (PS26/3) and came into force 31 March 2026. — [FCA CP25/27](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consultation/cp25-27.pdf); [Slaughter and May](https://www.slaughterandmay.com/insights/new-insights/fca-publishes-motor-finance-redress-scheme-policy-statement/); [FCA statement](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/statements/fca-confirms-motor-finance-redress-scheme)
- **Policy statement number:** inconsistent across secondary sources. Most say PS26/3; Freeths says PS26/31 and Lombard says PS23/6. — [Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/fca-publishes-final-rules-for-motor-finance-redress-scheme-what-s-changed-what-stays-the-same-and-what-firms-should-do-now/); [Lombard](https://www.lombard.co.uk/support/motor-finance-redress-scheme.html)
- **Size and scope:** £9.1bn and 12.1m eligible agreements, down from £11bn and 14.2m at consultation. — [Slaughter and May](https://www.slaughterandmay.com/insights/new-insights/fca-publishes-motor-finance-redress-scheme-policy-statement/)
  - Scheme 1 covers 6 April 2007 to 31 March 2014; Scheme 2 covers 1 April 2014 to 1 November 2024. It applies where the lender paid commission to the broker. — [Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/fca-publishes-final-rules-for-motor-finance-redress-scheme-what-s-changed-what-stays-the-same-and-what-firms-should-do-now/)
- **Average payout:** about £829, up from about £700 at consultation. — [SW Group](https://www.swgroup.com/insights-events/insights/the-motor-finance-redress-scheme-takes-shape/)
- **Exclusions:** consumers whose complaint was already decided by the Financial Ombudsman or a court, or who accepted redress. High-value loans are also excluded. — [SW Group](https://www.swgroup.com/insights-events/insights/the-motor-finance-redress-scheme-takes-shape/)
- **Original implementation dates:**
  - Scheme 2 contact from 30 June 2026; Scheme 1 from 31 August 2026.
  - Payment deadlines were November 2027 (Scheme 2) and January 2028 (Scheme 1).
  
  — [Lombard](https://www.lombard.co.uk/support/motor-finance-redress-scheme.html)
- **Legal challenge and suspension:**
  - On 1 May 2026 the FCA confirmed the schemes had been legally challenged. — [Lombard](https://www.lombard.co.uk/support/motor-finance-redress-scheme.html)
  - Three lenders and a consumer group challenged the scheme before the Upper Tribunal, on limitation periods and, in one case, interference with lenders' property rights under the Human Rights Act.
  - On 2 July 2026 the Upper Tribunal suspended parts of the scheme on terms agreed by the FCA with the challengers. Firms must comply with the unsuspended rules.
  - The hearing is likely to start 14 December 2026 or 16 February 2027.
  - Lenders need not calculate or pay compensation until the legal process concludes. The FCA expects payments to begin in 2027 if the scheme is upheld and not appealed.
  - Consumers can ask the Financial Ombudsman to review a lender's decision.
  
  — [FCA: Motor finance scheme partially suspended](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/statements/motor-finance-scheme-partially-suspended) (via search extract); [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/motor-finance-compensation-scheme-hanging-by-a-thread-amid-legal-row/); [Stephenson Harwood](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/insights/what-next-for-the-fca-s-motor-finance-compensation-scheme/)
- **Further delay possible:** a Treasury Committee member raised the prospect of appeals to the Court of Appeal and potentially the Supreme Court. — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/motor-finance-compensation-scheme-hanging-by-a-thread-amid-legal-row/)

### Inferences
- **For someone buying in October 2026:**
  - Agreements taken out after 1 November 2024 fall *outside* the scheme. A new agreement today would not benefit from it.
  - Protection now rests on the Supreme Court's s.140A "unfair relationship" test and FCA Consumer Duty rules.
  - The practical lesson is that dealers can still be paid commission by lenders. The dealer's APR may not be the best available, so compare it against a bank personal loan or an independent lender before signing.
- **Buyers can and should ask:**
  - "Is commission paid, how much, and is the dealer tied to this lender?"
  - Dealers must disclose the nature of commission. The FCA banned discretionary commission arrangements (where the dealer could raise the APR to increase its commission) from 28 January 2021. This is background knowledge; the FCA primary page was not fetched this session.
- **Existing PCP/HP holders** with 2007–2024 agreements should not pay a claims management company. The scheme is designed to be free, with lenders contacting customers. Payouts will be no earlier than 2027.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch FCA pages directly. The exact scope of what was suspended on 2 July 2026 versus what remains in force is only partially known.
- No 2026 primary source was retrieved on current commission disclosure requirements (post-Supreme Court FCA rule changes, if any). The DCA ban date is from background knowledge.
- The outcome of the Upper Tribunal challenge is pending as of 20 October 2026.

## 5. Hidden PCP traps: excess mileage, damage charges, voluntary termination, negative equity

### Takeaway
PCP's risks sit at the end of the deal:

- Excess mileage charges, typically about 7–11p per mile in contracts cited by consumers.
- Damage beyond "fair wear and tear".
- The voluntary termination (VT) right, which only lets you walk away free once 50% of the total amount payable has been paid, and still leaves excess-mileage and damage charges.
- Negative equity if the car is worth less than the settlement figure. That shortfall often gets rolled into the next deal.

### Cited Findings
- **Voluntary termination:** consumers commonly describe VT as available once half the total amount payable (deposit plus payments made) has been paid. Below that, the lender can pursue the difference. Fair wear and tear and excess-mileage charges still apply. — [MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5180860/pcp-voluntary-termination-excess-mileage) (consumer forum, anecdotal). The 50% rule itself is Consumer Credit Act 1974 s.99–100 (background knowledge; no primary source fetched).
- **Excess mileage on VT is disputed:** one poster says Financial Ombudsman complaints over excess-mileage charges on VT at over 50% are generally rejected. Others say the law is vague and agreement wording matters. — [MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6469806/excess-mileage-enforceable); [MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6177187/voluntary-termination-excess-mileage-and-debt-collector)
- **Excess-mileage rates** quoted in real contracts: 7.2p, 7.5p and 10.9p per mile (incl. VAT). Some are calculated pro rata on VT. — [MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5180860/pcp-voluntary-termination-excess-mileage). These are anecdotal and no manufacturer's current tariff was verified.
- **Damage:** damage beyond fair wear and tear can be charged on VT and on hand-back. — [MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5799340/pcp-voluntary-termination)
- **Settling avoids mileage disputes:** if you settle early or pay the GFV, the lender does not care about mileage. — [MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5180860/pcp-voluntary-termination-excess-mileage)
- **Negative equity:** when a deal is closed early, the shortfall is often rolled into the next deal, which adds interest. — [MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6111113/pcp-advice) (anecdotal)

### Inferences
- **Worked mileage example (illustrative):** a 4-year PCP contracted at 8,000 miles/year (32,000 total) but driven 10,000/year (40,000) gives 8,000 excess miles. At 7.5p per mile that is £600; at 10.9p it is £872. This is only payable if the car is handed back.
- **VT on the worked example (9.9% PCP):** total amount payable is £15,571, so the 50% threshold is about £7,786. With a £1,250 deposit plus £207.20 per month, that is reached around month 32 of 48.
- **Negative equity is most likely early** in a PCP on a used car, when the outstanding balance (including the balloon) exceeds the car's value. Buyers who may change cars within 1–2 years face the greatest risk.
- Under-declaring mileage to cut the monthly payment is a false economy if the car is handed back. It is irrelevant if the buyer plans to pay the balloon or settle.

- No current manufacturer excess-mileage pence-per-mile figures were retrieved for approved-used PCP.
- No 2026 data on the prevalence of negative equity was found.

## 6. Can a buyer take dealer finance to get a discount or deposit contribution, then settle early?

### Takeaway
Often yes, and it is the main way finance can beat cash. The buyer takes a finance-linked deposit contribution or discount, then settles early using the statutory right to settle at any time. The cost is a small early-settlement interest charge, usually up to about a month's interest.

The risks:
- Some offers include clawback or minimum-term conditions.
- Dealers may withdraw the discount if they suspect early settlement.
- Currently advertised *used*-car contributions are small (£250–£500).

### Cited Findings
- **Available contributions:** finance-linked deposit contributions on approved-used cars as of October 2026 are £250 (VW Solutions PCP, 13–36-month-old cars) and £500 (Kia EV6 Approved Used). — [VW](https://www.volkswagen.co.uk/en/used-cars/approved-used-offers.html); [Kia UK](https://www.kia.com/uk/used-cars/used-car-offers/ev6/)
- **New-car contributions are larger:** £3,000–£5,750 at Toyota. — [Toyota UK current offers](https://www.toyota.co.uk/current-offers)
- **Settling avoids end-of-contract charges:** settling early or paying the GFV removes mileage concerns. — [MSE Forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5180860/pcp-voluntary-termination-excess-mileage)

### Inferences
- **Worked example (researcher calculation):** a £12,500 car with a £1,000 deposit contribution, £1,250 customer deposit and 9.9% APR, settled after one month, costs about £11,661 in total. That is about £840 below the cash price.
- With the actual £250–£500 used-car contributions found, the net gain from settling early is about £150–£450 after a month's interest.
- **Practical tips:**
  - Get the contribution and any discount written into the order and finance agreement.
  - Check for any clawback clause or minimum-period requirement before settling.
  - Request a settlement figure in writing.
  - Settle within the 14-day withdrawal window, or soon after. Withdrawing within 14 days under the Consumer Credit Act means repaying the credit plus interest accrued. Whether the contribution survives withdrawal depends on the dealer's terms (background knowledge; not verified this session).
- **Credit-file note:** taking finance adds a hard search and a short-lived account to the buyer's credit file. This is minor for most people.
