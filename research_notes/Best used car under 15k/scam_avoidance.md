## 1. What are the most common current UK used-car scams, and how prevalent are they?

### Takeaway
Fake listings / deposit scams on social media (mainly Meta platforms) are the single biggest volume threat and vehicles were the largest purchase-scam category reported to Lloyds in 2025; history problems (outstanding finance, mileage discrepancies, write-offs, cloning) are very common in the cars that get checked. Vehicle-specific Action Fraud figures for 2025/26 were not found; the latest vehicle-specific Action Fraud number located is from 2021.

### Cited Findings

**Fake listings, deposit and escrow scams (Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree, etc.)**
- Lloyds' year-end 2025 analysis: vehicle and ticket scams dominated UK purchase-fraud reports in 2025 — [Crowdfund Insider, Dec 2025](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/12/256857-major-uk-purchase-scams-examined-in-year-end-lloyds-bank-report/)
- Vehicle and vehicle-accessory scams were the largest category of purchase scams reported to Lloyds in 2025, and more than 70% of purchase scams started on Meta-owned platforms (Facebook, Instagram, Marketplace) — [Jalopnik, citing Lloyds data](https://www.jalopnik.com/2236283/facebook-marketplace-reverse-image-search-why/) (secondary source; Lloyds' own release not read)
- Earlier baseline: Lloyds data showed vehicle scams up 74% in H1 2023 vs prior year, average loss £998 per victim, and more than two-thirds of car and van scams reported to Lloyds were advertised on Meta-owned platforms — [Which?, 2023](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/scam-alert-vehicle-buying-scams-on-the-rise-akFam6i0HP0X); [LBC](https://lbc.co.uk/tech/fdf8f4955e8a40b68bce1a5b32035894)
- Typical method: criminals post non-existent vehicles using pictures of genuine cars, ask for a deposit or full payment, and give reasons why the buyer can't view first — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/scam-alert-vehicle-buying-scams-on-the-rise-akFam6i0HP0X)
- UK Finance's annual fraud report (published 2026, covering 2025): purchase scams were 71% of APP scam cases, with losses up 20% to £118.1m (all goods, not vehicles alone). UK Finance called for marketplaces to verify sellers and keep payments in-platform rather than allowing off-site bank transfers — [Crowdfund Insider, June 2026](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/06/285657-fraudulent-activities-and-sophisticated-scams-continue-to-pose-serious-threat-to-uk-security-as-losses-approach-1-3b/)
- Older Action Fraud data: almost 3,000 online vehicle fraud reports in 2021 (up 21% since 2019), costing £9.5m — [Get Safe Online](https://www.getsafeonline.org/news/online-vehicle-fraud-increasing-buyers-sellers-take-care); [This is Lancashire](https://www.thisislancashire.co.uk/news/23032928.uk-drivers-warned-car-scam-costing-motorists-thousands/)
- Fake escrow / "holding account" scam: fraudsters impersonate an established escrow provider (Action Fraud cites fake Amazon-branded "holding accounts" — Amazon offers no such service) to persuade buyers to pay by bank transfer before seeing the car, often claiming a "cooling-off period" to reclaim money — [Action Fraud: fake holding accounts](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk/news/fake-holding-accounts-target-car-buyers-online); [Action Fraud: buyers beware](https://actionfraud.police.uk/news/buyers-beware-of-online-car-scam)
- False adverts often price cars well below market value — a key warning sign — [Action Fraud](https://actionfraud.police.uk/news/buyers-beware-of-online-car-scam)

**Outstanding finance, clocking, write-offs, stolen (HPI self-reported, dates unclear)**
- HPI says about 1 in 3 vehicles it checks has an active finance agreement recorded against it (the finance company may still legally own the car) — [HPI](https://hpicheck.com/car-history-check); [Express & Star / syndicated](https://www.expressandstar.com/recommended/hpi-usedcars-finance-mileage-writeoffs-8759548). Compare 1 in 7 in 2004 — [AM Online, 2004](https://am-online.com/news/2004/10/19/1-in-3-vehicles-hit-anti-fraud-register-says-hpi/7195)
- HPI: 1 in 11 vehicles checked shows a mileage discrepancy (up from 1 in 14 in 2019, 1 in 16 in 2017, 1 in 20 in 2014) — [HPI](https://hpicheck.com/car-history-check); [Fleet News, 2019](https://fleetnews.co.uk/news/fleet-industry-news/2019/09/18/vehicles-most-likely-to-be-clocked-revealed-by-hpi)
- HPI says its checks uncover 2,205 insurance write-offs per day (another HPI page says 2,001) and 109 stolen vehicles per day — [HPI](https://hpicheck.com/car-history-check); [hpi.co.uk](https://hpi.co.uk/hpi-car-check)
- Caveat: these are proportions of cars *checked* (self-selected, often suspicious) and are HPI marketing figures, not population rates.
- Experian: 2013 release said more than 1 in 4 cars checked by dealers had live finance — [AM Online, 2013](https://www.am-online.com/news/2013/2/5/experian-launches-real-time-finance-status-tool/32323/) (old; no current Experian figure found)

**Cloned cars**
- RAC FOI analysis: DVLA was contacted in 2025 by 11,402 drivers about fines for vehicles they didn't recognise (+9% on 2024's 10,460; +93% on 2018's 5,912) — a record high — [RAC](https://www.rac.co.uk/drive/news/motoring-news/cloned-vehicles-rise-to-record-high/); separate FOI (Select Car Leasing) put suspected cloned cases at 11,395 in 2025 vs 7,430 in 2021 — [Fleet News](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/vehicle-cloning-reaches-record-levels-data-suggests). These are reports, including ANPR misreads, not confirmed crimes.
- HPI: cloning is typically used to disguise a stolen car; innocent buyers lose both the car (returned to legal owner) and their money. HPI's check compares reg, engine number and VIN with DVLA records — [Body Shop Mag / HPI](https://www.bodyshopmag.com/?p=27312)
- APPG for Transport Safety (Dec 2025) estimated ~1 in 15 UK vehicles carries an illegal plate of some kind (cloned, ghost, stealth, 3D/4D) — [EasyReg, citing APPG](https://www.easy-reg.co.uk/blog/cloned-plates-innocent-drivers-fined) (secondary)

**Write-offs (Cat S / Cat N)**
- Four categories: A and B can never legally return to the road; Cat S = structural damage (chassis, suspension) and must be re-registered with DVLA after repair; Cat N = non-structural damage, no re-registration but DVLA must be told — [Carbuyer](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/car-buying/114820/cat-s-and-cat-n-cars-explained-guide-to-buying-an-insurance-write-off); [Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/car-news/consumer-news/99750/cat-a-b-s-and-n-cars-new-insurance-write-off-categories-from-october)
- Cat N does not mean minor: can include brakes, steering, airbags — [Start Rescue](https://www.startrescue.co.uk/breakdown-cover/motoring-advice/safety-and-security/car-write-offs-cat-a-b-s-n-explained)
- Cat S/N replaced Cat C/D in October 2017; older C/D cars weren't reassessed and are still on sale — [Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/car-news/consumer-news/99750/cat-a-b-s-and-n-cars-new-insurance-write-off-categories-from-october)

**Curbstoners / disguised traders posing as private sellers**
- It is an offence under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 to falsely create the impression a trader isn't acting in the course of business; dealers must label adverts as "trade" — [Highland Council Trading Standards](https://www.highland.gov.uk/news/article/2243/trading_standards_clamp_down_on_disguised_car_dealers); [Car Dealer Magazine](https://cardealermagazine.co.uk/car-dealer-ordered-pay-1500-duping-naive-customer/126897)
- National Trading Standards: a Facebook probe in Wales found many "private" sellers were actually motor traders; problem described as widespread — [Car Dealer Magazine](https://cardealermagazine.co.uk/publish/?p=297023)
- Example penalties: NI hidden dealer who met buyer at a roundabout fined £5,000 + £1,000 compensation; Middlesbrough dealer fined £200, buyer received £1,910 compensation — [Car Dealer Magazine](https://cardealermagazine.co.uk/publish/?p=82050); [Armagh i (clocked car case)](https://armaghi.com/news/man-fined-5000-for-selling-clocked-car-by-at-least-32500-miles/28440)
- Red flags: V5C address doesn't match where you meet/seller's home; seller has many other cars listed; meeting in car parks/lay-bys — [Highland Council](https://www.highland.gov.uk/news/article/2243/trading_standards_clamp_down_on_disguised_car_dealers)

### Inferences
- For a £10k–£15k buyer, the two highest-impact risks are (a) losing a deposit/full payment by bank transfer to a fake listing, and (b) buying a car with hidden finance, clocked mileage, write-off history or a cloned identity. Both are largely preventable by (i) never paying before seeing the car and its documents in person at the keeper's address, and (ii) running a paid history check.
- In this price band, a disguised trader is a meaningful risk because it strips the buyer of Consumer Rights Act protection; Trading Standards examples skew to cheaper cars (~£500–£2,000), so prevalence at £10k+ is less clear.

### Gaps
- No 2025/2026 Action Fraud vehicle-specific report counts or losses found (latest: 2021). No 2025 Lloyds average loss per vehicle scam (latest: £998, H1 2023).
- No current Experian AutoCheck statistics found.
- No reliable UK data found on "VAT / overseas seller" scams specifically (e.g., "I'm abroad / in the army, car will be shipped via courier"); only general descriptions of "can't view" excuses.
- HPI ratios are of cars checked, not all used cars; reporting periods unclear.

## 2. Which free checks exist, and which paid checks are worth it (and costs)?

### Takeaway
Free gov.uk checks (DVLA vehicle enquiry, MOT history with recorded mileages, recall check) catch identity mismatches and many clocking cases; a paid history check (~£7–£20) is the only practical way to see outstanding finance, write-off status and stolen markers, and is worth it for any £10k+ purchase.

### Cited Findings
- gov.uk's official checklist "Check a used vehicle you're buying" covers: matching seller's details with DVLA records, MOT history, and a recall check (step four) — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/checks-when-buying-a-used-car)
- DVLA vehicle enquiry service (vehicleenquiry.service.gov.uk) confirms recorded vehicle details and can confirm whether the V5C shown is the latest issued — [HPI paperwork guide](https://hpicheck.com/car-buyers-guide/guides/the-paperwork); [Growth Hub guide](https://heygrowthhub.com/business-information/checks-when-buying-a-used-car)
- MOT history: check recorded mileage at each MOT, not just the current reading, to spot clocking — [HPI paperwork guide](https://www.hpi.co.uk/buyers-guide/guides/the-paperwork.html)
- gov.uk recall checker: free, by registration (or make/model/year), shows unfixed safety recalls — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/check-if-a-vehicle-has-been-recalled). A Luton car trader was sentenced for selling cars without disclosing safety recalls — [Luton Council](https://m.luton.gov.uk/Page/Show/news/Pages/Car-trader-sentenced-for-selling-cars-without-disclosing-safety-recalls.aspx)
- Paid check prices (Feb 2026 comparisons; verify before buying):
  - HPI single check £19.99; 3-check bundle £29.97 — [CarAnalytics, Feb 2026](https://www.caranalytics.co.uk/guides/who-has-the-cheapest-hpi-check/); [Green Flag comparison](https://vehiclecheck.greenflag.com/hpi-check-comparison)
  - Green Flag full check £9.99 — [Green Flag](https://vehiclecheck.greenflag.com/hpi-check-comparison)
  - CarAnalytics basic £6.99, full £14.99 — [CarAnalytics, Feb 2026](https://www.caranalytics.co.uk/guides/5-best-vehicle-check-services-when-buying-used-cars-in-the-uk/)
  - Total Car Check: conflicting figures (£8.99 single / £19.99 for five in an older review vs £1.99–£3.99 in an undated forum post) — [Solent Renegades](https://www.solent-renegades.co.uk/threads/best-car-check-websites.44476); [Autodoc forum](https://club.autodoc.co.uk/forum/topic/best-hpi-check)
  - The AA offers a vehicle check product — [The AA car check](https://www.theaa.com/car-check) (price not captured)
- HPI-style checks verify reg/engine number/VIN against DVLA records and cover finance, write-off, stolen and mileage markers — [Body Shop Mag / HPI](https://www.bodyshopmag.com/?p=27312); [Total Car Check important data checks](https://totalcarcheck.co.uk/ImportantDataChecks)

### Inferences
- Note that CarAnalytics and Green Flag are vendors comparing themselves against HPI; their pricing comparisons are self-interested. HPI's selling point is its finance-data guarantee (not verified here).
- Practical order: run free gov.uk checks on every shortlisted car (minutes, zero cost); run one paid full check on the car you intend to buy, as close to purchase day as possible (finance can be added later).

## 3. Legal protections (CRA 2015, private sales, distance selling, Section 75, finance)

### Takeaway
Buying from a dealer (ideally with part payment on a credit card or via a regulated finance agreement) gives far stronger protection than a private sale: 30-day right to reject, 6-month presumption of fault, 14-day cancellation for fully remote purchases, and joint liability of card/finance providers. Private sales give only limited protection, and Section 75 usually won't apply.

### Cited Findings
- Consumer Rights Act 2015 applies to dealer sales, not private sales — [Honest John](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/how-to-reject-a-car-your-consumer-rights)
- Short-term right to reject: 30 days to reject an unsatisfactory car for a full refund — [Honest John](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/how-to-reject-a-car-your-consumer-rights); [Auto Trader help](https://help.autotrader.co.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/13622917736221-What-are-my-rights-if-I-need-to-return-a-car)
- 30 days to 6 months: entitled to repair or replacement (then refund/price reduction if that fails); fault presumed present at purchase unless dealer proves otherwise; dealer may deduct for usage on a refund after failed repair — [Honest John](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/how-to-reject-a-car-your-consumer-rights)
- Escalation routes: Citizens Advice, The Motor Ombudsman, Trading Standards — [Honest John](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/how-to-reject-a-car-your-consumer-rights)
- Distance selling: Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 give 14 days from delivery to return a car bought wholly online/remotely for a full refund, no reason needed; cancel in writing; remotely paid deposits refundable; if not told of the right, cancellation period extends up to 12 months — [The Motor Ombudsman](https://www.themotorombudsman.org/knowledge/can-i-return-my-car-within-14-days-of-buying-it/); [Motor Ombudsman distance-sales FAQs](https://www.themotorombudsman.org/distance-sales-faqs)
- The right does not apply if you visit the dealer to collect, pay the deposit or sign paperwork — [Motorway](https://motorway.co.uk/sell-my-car/guides/is-there-a-cooling-off-period-when-buying-a-car)
- Finance agreements carry their own separate 14-day cooling-off period — [Motorway](https://motorway.co.uk/sell-my-car/guides/is-there-a-cooling-off-period-when-buying-a-car)
- Section 75 (Consumer Credit Act 1974): card provider jointly liable for misrepresentation/breach where the cash price is over £100 and not more than £30,000; FOS confirms it applies even if only part (e.g., deposit) was paid on the card — [Zable](https://app.zable.co.uk/cards/how-do-credit-cards-work/can-you-buy-a-car-with-a-credit-card)
- Section 75 limits: needs a direct debtor-creditor-supplier chain; a claim failed where the card payment went to an auction house rather than the seller; banks have rejected claims on private sales — [FOS decisions](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5684926.pdf); [FOS](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5762114.pdf)
- Private sale: in an FOS case the seller wasn't acting in the course of business, so the buyer had only limited protection under the CRA 2015 / Sale of Goods Act 1979 — [FOS decision](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5684926.pdf)
- Trading Standards: the law on private sales "does not provide anything like the level of protection" of dealer sales — [Highland Council](https://www.highland.gov.uk/news/article/2243/trading_standards_clamp_down_on_disguised_car_dealers)
- Hire purchase / PCP: the finance company is the supplier, so CRA satisfactory-quality claims run against the lender; Section 56 CCA makes the lender liable for what the broker/dealer said before the agreement — [FOS DRN-5368399](https://financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5368399.pdf); [FOS DRN-5931233](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5931233.pdf)
- APP (bank transfer) scam reimbursement: mandatory scheme since 7 Oct 2024; max £85,000 per claim; claim within 13 months; firms may apply up to £100 excess; exclusions for complicity or gross negligence (firm must show customer ignored PSR "requirements") — [Money to the Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/banking/what-are-the-new-app-fraud-reimbursement-rules); [Financial Ombudsman](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/businesses/complaints-deal/fraud-scams/app-fraud-scams-involving-authorised-payments)
- PSR data: Oct 2024–Jun 2025, £112m reimbursed, 88% of claimed losses returned; purchase fraud ~60% of APP cases; only ~2% of claims rejected for failing the consumer standard of caution; independent evaluation due Q2 2026 — [Payment Expert / A&O Shearman summaries](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/uk-psr-publishes-one-year-impact-of-app-reimburse); [Payment Expert](https://paymentexpert.com/2025/05/16/psr-success-app-fraud-reimbursement/)

### Inferences
- For a £10k–£15k car, paying even a small deposit (e.g., £100+) on a credit card to a dealer brings the whole price within Section 75 (car price under £30k). This is the single cheapest legal upgrade a buyer can make.
- Bank-transfer losses to scammers are now usually reimbursable (up to £85k), but this is a safety net, not a plan: ignoring bank warnings could be argued as gross negligence, and payments by card are outside the APP scheme (covered by chargeback/S75 instead).

## 4. Inspection advice: V5C, service history, independent inspections, test drive

### Takeaway
Verify the car's identity (V5C vs DVLA vs VIN on the car), view at the registered keeper's address, check MOT mileage trail and service records, and for a £10k–£15k purchase consider an independent inspection (~£99–£339 with the AA).

### Cited Findings
- V5C: hold up to light to check the DVLA watermark; check serial number isn't in known stolen-blank ranges; most V5Cs are now red; use DVLA enquiry to confirm it is the latest issued — [HPI paperwork guide](https://hpicheck.com/car-buyers-guide/guides/the-paperwork); [Growth Hub guide](https://heygrowthhub.com/business-information/checks-when-buying-a-used-car)
- The registered keeper on the V5C is not necessarily the legal owner (finance companies may own the car) — [HPI paperwork guide](https://hpicheck.com/car-buyers-guide/guides/the-paperwork)
- Check that the V5C address matches the seller's address (disguised trader / stolen car indicator) — [Highland Council Trading Standards](https://www.highland.gov.uk/news/article/2243/trading_standards_clamp_down_on_disguised_car_dealers)
- Check that the MOT history matches what the seller told you, including year-by-year mileage — [HPI](https://www.hpi.co.uk/buyers-guide/guides/the-paperwork.html)
- AA pre-purchase inspection prices (current AA site): Basic £99 members / £110 non-members; Comprehensive £197–£287 members / £219–£319 non-members; Advanced £251–£305 members / £279–£339 non-members — [The AA vehicle inspections](https://www.theaa.com/vehicle-inspections/); [AA FAQs](https://www.theaa.com/vehicle-inspections/faqs.html)
- RAC: only older prices found — Essentials from £149, enhanced £194 (1400cc, undated) — [LoveMoney](https://www.lovemoney.com/news/amp/12467/a-clever-way-to-save-a-packet-on-a-secondhand-car); a competitor citing Feb 2024 prices lists RAC at £116–£267 — [ClickMechanic](https://www.clickmechanic.com/partners/the-car-expert-pre-purchase-inspection)
- DEKRA (dated): £99–£119 Peace of Mind; £145–£229 Comprehensive — [LoveMoney](https://www.lovemoney.com/news/amp/12467/a-clever-way-to-save-a-packet-on-a-secondhand-car)
- AA used-car buyers' guide exists as a reference — [The AA](https://www.theaa.com/vehicle-inspections/used-car-buyers-guide)

### Inferences
- At £10k–£15k an inspection costing ~1–2% of price is proportionate, especially for private sales where there's little legal comeback.
- Physically check VIN on the car (windscreen/door pillar/stamped) matches the V5C and history check, as cloned cars carry matching plates but often mismatched VIN/engine numbers.

## 5. Safe payment practices

### Takeaway
Never pay anything (including a "holding deposit") before seeing the car and V5C in person at the keeper's address; never use third-party "escrow" or "holding accounts" suggested by the seller; prefer card for deposits with dealers; for bank transfers, use Confirmation of Payee and treat any non-match as a stop sign.

### Cited Findings
- Action Fraud: meet the seller face to face and see the vehicle before transferring any money; check seller's phone/email online; beware below-market prices — [Action Fraud](https://actionfraud.police.uk/news/buyers-beware-of-online-car-scam)
- Fake escrow/holding accounts are a known car scam; Amazon provides no such service — [Action Fraud](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk/news/fake-holding-accounts-target-car-buyers-online)
- Citizens Advice suggests a credit card gives better protection against shopping fraud — [Action Fraud / Citizens Advice, via search summary](https://actionfraud.police.uk/news/buyers-beware-of-online-car-scam)
- Confirmation of Payee returns: match (proceed); close match (shows actual name); no match (contact the payee); unavailable. For a private seller, use first and last name; for a business, the name on the business account — [UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/policy-and-guidance/guidance/confirmation-payee)
- APP reimbursement covers Faster Payments/CHAPS only (not card); £85k cap; 13-month claim window — [Money to the Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/banking/what-are-the-new-app-fraud-reimbursement-rules)
- UK Finance urges marketplaces to keep payments in-platform rather than off-site bank transfers, a common starting point for purchase scams — [Crowdfund Insider, June 2026](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/06/285657-fraudulent-activities-and-sophisticated-scams-continue-to-pose-serious-threat-to-uk-security-as-losses-approach-1-3b/)

### Inferences
- Suggested sequence for a private sale: view at V5C address → check V5C/VIN/history → agree price → pay by bank transfer on the spot with CoP "match" against the V5C keeper's name (CoP name matching the V5C name is a useful cross-check) → get a signed receipt → complete V5C new keeper section online with DVLA.
- For dealer sales: pay deposit by credit card (S75), balance by debit card/finance/bank transfer to the dealer's verified business account.

### Gaps
- No source found on how CoP results specifically interact with APP reimbursement decisions for car purchases.
- Cash-handling/banker's draft risk guidance (e.g., fake banker's drafts) not researched.

## Prioritised checklist (synthesised from findings above, for the report writer)
1. Shortlist only from sources where you can view in person; treat below-market prices, "can't view", "seller abroad/shipping", and requests for deposits or escrow as scams (Action Fraud, Which?/Lloyds).
2. Run free checks on the reg: DVLA vehicle enquiry (make/colour/V5C issue date), MOT history (mileage trail, advisories), recall check (gov.uk).
3. Prefer a dealer (CRA 2015 30-day reject / 6-month presumption); if "private", check V5C address = seller address and that they aren't selling many cars (disguised trader).
4. Buy a full paid history check (~£7–£20) on the final car: finance, write-off (Cat S/N), stolen, mileage, plate changes.
5. At viewing: V5C watermark and keeper details; VIN on car = V5C = history check; service history; test drive; consider AA/RAC inspection (£99–£339).
6. Pay safely: never before viewing; credit card for dealer deposit (S75 up to £30k); CoP on bank transfers; never use seller-nominated escrow; know APP reimbursement (£85k cap, 13 months).
7. Distance purchase from a dealer: 14-day cancellation from delivery if fully remote (Motor Ombudsman); finance has its own 14-day cooling-off.
